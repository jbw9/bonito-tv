A home screen for the whole room

Bonito swaps your TV's home screen for one with arcade and party games, your phone as the remote, and a Spotify queue your friends can add to. It's free, open source, and takes your TV from switched on to game night in one button press.

Put it on your TV

Which TV do you have? If you use Claude Code, it can do the whole install for you. Otherwise the steps are below.

Everything works: it becomes your home screen, switches HDMI inputs, opens Netflix and your other apps, and plays YouTube without ads. Samsung makes you sign apps you install yourself, so the first install takes a while: about 40 minutes by hand, less with Claude Code.

With Claude Code

Paste this into Claude Code on a Mac or PC that's on the same Wi-Fi as the TV. It sets up the tools, finds your TV and installs, and tells you which buttons to press on the TV remote.

Install Bonito on my Samsung TV. Clone https://github.com/jbw9/bonito-tv and follow SETUP.md in it from start to finish. Do every step you can yourself, and tell me exactly what to press on the TV when you need me.

No Claude Code yet? Install it from claude.com/claude-code, open Terminal, type claude and paste.

By hand

  1. Turn on Developer Mode. On the TV, open Apps and press 1 2 3 4 5 on the remote. Switch Developer Mode on, type your computer's IP address, and restart the TV.
  2. Install Tizen Studio on your computer and create a Samsung certificate in its Certificate Manager. You'll need a free Samsung account.
  3. Download this project and run ./deploy.sh <your TV's IP>. It installs and opens Bonito.

The full walkthrough has every click and fixes for the usual snags. Just want to try it first? Open your TV's Internet app and go to jbw9.github.io/bonito-tv/tv.

Nearly everything works: games, phone remote, music and opening your other apps. HDMI switching is Samsung only. Works on Google TV Streamer, Chromecast with Google TV, Nvidia Shield, Sony and TCL Google TVs, and other Android TVs.

By hand, on the TV alone

  1. Unlock developer options. Go to Settings › System › About and press Android TV OS build seven times.
  2. Get Downloader. Install the free Downloader app from Google Play, then allow it under Settings › Apps › Security & restrictions › Unknown sources.
  3. Download Bonito. In Downloader, type jbw9.github.io/bonito-tv/bonito.apk and press Install.
  4. Open it from your apps. Long-press it to move it to the front of the row.

Nearly everything works: games, phone remote, music and opening your other apps. HDMI switching is Samsung only. Works on Fire TV Sticks, Fire TV Cube and Fire TV smart TVs.

By hand, on the TV alone

  1. Unlock developer options. Go to Settings › My Fire TV › About and press your device's name seven times.
  2. Get Downloader. Install Downloader from the Appstore, then turn it on under Settings › My Fire TV › Developer options › Install unknown apps.
  3. Download Bonito. In Downloader, type jbw9.github.io/bonito-tv/bonito.apk and press Install.
  4. Open it from Your Apps & Channels.

The games, phone remote, leaderboards and the music jam work in your TV's own web browser. Nothing to install. It can't open other apps or switch inputs from there. This works on LG, Samsung, Philips and any other TV with a browser, and on a laptop plugged in with HDMI.

On the TV

  1. Open the TV's web browser. On LG it's called Web Browser, on Samsung Internet.
  2. Go to jbw9.github.io/bonito-tv/tv and bookmark it.
  3. Scan the code under Connect Phone with your phone, and you have a remote with a keyboard.

On a computer plugged into the TV

  1. Plug in with HDMI and open jbw9.github.io/bonito-tv/tv in Chrome.
  2. Go full screen with F11, or Control-Command-F on a Mac.
  3. Put the laptop away. Your phone does the rest.

Apple TV and Roku don't allow apps like this, and neither has a web browser. The easiest way in is a Google TV Streamer or Chromecast with Google TV plugged into a spare HDMI port, which runs everything. Or plug in any laptop and follow the browser steps.

Then make it yours from your phone. On the TV, open Settings › Phone setup and scan the code. Your phone sets your name, city, design and Spotify login. There are no files to edit.

Questions

Could my phone end up controlling someone else's TV?

No. Each TV makes up its own random room code the first time it starts, and a phone only joins the room in the QR code it scanned. The code is the only way to find a TV, and the songs and requests in a room can only be seen by phones that know it.

What do I need for the music?

Spotify Premium. Spotify only lets home-made apps like this play music if you register your own free developer app. It takes about two minutes, and the phone setup page walks you through it. Your login is stored on your TV, nowhere else.

How do I undo it?

Uninstall it like any other app. On Samsung, highlight it in Apps and choose Delete, and turn Developer Mode off if you like. On Google TV, Android TV and Fire TV, uninstall it from Settings › Apps. Nothing else on the TV is changed.

What does it store, and where?

High scores with the name you type, and the songs friends request, in a shared database. Requests are deleted after a day. Your settings and Spotify login stay on the TV.

Does it cost anything?

No. It's free and open source under the MIT license. No ads, no account.

Can I change it?

Please do. Fork it on GitHub, and if it's something others would like, send a pull request. The designs live in app/js/home.js, each game is one file in app/js/, and you can add your own apps to the home screen in app/config.local.js.
